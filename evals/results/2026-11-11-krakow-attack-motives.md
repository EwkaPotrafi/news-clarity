**Verdict:** Any report that claims to know the motive, whether far-right or left-wing, is **unverifiable** at this point. Prosecutors say the motive has not been established, and both political camps have gone further than the evidence supports.

**In one sentence:** On 7 November, a 24-year-old Polish man shot people at random in Kraków's Main Market Square after apparently killing his own family. He died in hospital on 9 November, and why he did it is still unknown.

**What we know for sure**
- Six people were killed in Kraków. His father, mother and sister were found dead at the family home in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski, and officials believe he killed them too. (Reuters via Devdiscourse)
- The attacker was Michał Oksiński, 24. He is Polish, as police confirmed. Online claims that he was an immigrant or Ukrainian were false. (Demagog, OKO.press)
- Two AR-15 rifles and two pistols were found in his car and on him, along with several hundred rounds of ammunition. According to prosecutors, he had held a permit for one rifle and one pistol since January. (TVN24)
- Prosecutors provisionally classified the case as terrorist acts, but the motive has not been established. (Super Express)
- He died in hospital on Monday. That makes the motive harder to establish, because he can no longer be questioned.

**What is claimed but not confirmed**
- **That he had far-right views.** Interior Minister Kierwiński said preliminary findings suggested he was "fascinated by far-right ideologies". A specialist website reported that he appeared to wear a Falanga symbol during the attack. On 10 November, Justice Minister Żurek told Radio Kraków that his computer showed interest in websites about Anders Breivik. These are the strongest leads, but none of them has been presented as evidence of motive.
- **That he was left-wing.** Right-leaning outlets cite a Facebook profile, now deleted, said to show a Pride flag and mockery of Christianity and Pope John Paul II. Even the outlet making this argument doesn't verify that the profile was his.
- **That the government deleted his account to hide evidence.** Spanish MEP Hermann Tertsch and others claim this. No evidence has been offered.
- **Russian involvement.** Prosecutors say they will examine every thread, including a Russian one, but there is no evidence of foreign-service involvement so far.

**How different sides see it**
- **The government and left-leaning media:** The attack was carefully prepared, with several weapons and a Falanga-type emblem, and he was interested in Breivik. That looks like far-right lone-wolf terrorism, and saying so early helps the public understand the threat. OKO.press notes the method resembles far-right attacks, though it doesn't rule out other explanations.
- **The opposition and right-leaning media:** The interior minister labelled the attacker politically before there was evidence, and that cast suspicion on the whole right. Law and Justice leader Jarosław Kaczyński called those claims a "blatant lie". A lawyer quoted by Super Express argues that the statement imposed a political narrative before investigators established a motive.
- **Cautious experts:** Killing his own family first and then strangers at random may point to personal collapse rather than ideology. Terrorism expert Robert Borkowski said it looked more like the work of a frustrated mass murderer than an ideological attack.

**Watch the wording**
- "Far-right terrorist" or "leftist Antifa sympathiser" → **"an attacker whose motive is under investigation"**
- "Debunked" (said about the minister's claim) → **"disputed, not yet confirmed or disproven"**
- "Evidence vanished" → **"a profile attributed to him is no longer online"**
- "Terrorist attack" is the formal legal classification for now, not a finding about his ideology.

**Missing context**
- The government has already pulled back: its spokesperson Adam Szłapka said officials should not comment until the facts are clear. Few partisan posts mention this.
- Some motives are never found. In a 2023 shooting on the same street in Kraków, prosecutors never established the motive. (Bezprawnik)
- Early reports said five dead and gave his age as 25. These were corrected later, so older articles you come across may conflict.
- The flag found in his room was a Gadsden flag, an American libertarian symbol. Prosecutors noted it is not widely linked to extremism, so it doesn't fit neatly with either side's story.

**Pragmatic take:** The facts of the attack are solid. The motive is not. The far-right lead currently has more official backing, from the minister's statement, the reported emblem and the Breivik searches. The left-wing lead rests on a profile nobody has verified. Neither is proven, and investigators are now focusing on whether anyone helped or inspired him. **Confidence that the motive is known: low.** That would change if prosecutors release what they find on his four phones and computer, a manifesto or messages, or information about contacts. In the meantime, the sensible thing is not to share posts that claim to know.
